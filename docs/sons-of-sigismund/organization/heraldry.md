# 2.3 Heraldry and Honors

**The Sons of Sigismund are Black Templars**, not a separately heralded successor Chapter. Their armor preserves the familiar Black Templars language of **black plate, white pauldrons and the cross**. The household's distinguishing *earned* distinction is the right of Sword Brethren to display **yellow in honor of Sigismund and the Imperial Fists lineage**.

## 2.3.1 The Black Templars cross

The Black Templars cross remains the principal warrior insignia. The stylized shield on the *Williams Warhammer Lore* website is **an editorial illustration**, not a formally approved new chapter badge for the Sons.

The exact design and placement of squad identifiers, campaign honors, and any personalized variations **remain open**. Don't interpret site decorations as miniature painting requirements.

## 2.3.2 The right of yellow

Successful completion of the **Trial of Swords** grants Sword Brother status and the **lifelong, optional heraldic privilege of yellow**. It may appear as a yellow pauldron with a black cross, a yellow cross on a white pauldron, a single greave or knee panel, a helmet stripe or another discreet detail.

The privilege is **earned, not compulsory**. A Sword Brother who chooses not to wear yellow has not lost his status. A brother who later becomes a Marshal, Castellan, Chaplain or other officer retains the right if he previously earned it.

**Neophytes and Initiates have not earned this yellow honor.** Yellow isn't automatically a universal rank-color code for every position.

## 2.3.3 Personal honors and decorations

Approved visual direction includes **battle-worn black armor and white shoulders**, with room for individually chosen devotional parchment, bronze details, crimson cloth, iron, relic casings and scratches. These are **painting and personalization options**, not a mandatory rank hierarchy.

A warrior's armor can eventually display his **oaths, campaign participation and stewardship of relics**, but those markings must only record accepted lore or actual played engagements. The number of relics carried is **not** a measure of rank.
