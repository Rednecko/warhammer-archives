# 7.4 Brotherhood Deck and Upper Launch Galleries

<div class="archive-page-label">The Righteous Crusader · Principal Astartes living and training level</div>

<span class="archive-status">Residential and circulation concept recorded</span> <span class="archive-status open">Actual occupancy and future 20-Marine allocation under review</span>

Descending below the sanctified command chambers, the order reaches its **inhabited heart**. This level is not another cathedral, but a lived-in complex of barracks, personal armories, shared meals, veteran residences, practical drills and access to the launch decks. It is where the Sons of Sigismund return from campaigns, maintain weapons, mentor Neophytes and study their own history. The principal **central corridor** serves the four mirrored Brotherhood complexes, two veteran wings and, at the far end, the **Great Brotherhood Hall**.

This is one of the cruiser’s **widest planned regions**. At its outer flanks lie separate allied accommodation wings and aircraft servicing spaces. The great hangar also cuts vertically through the ship: two Thunderhawk positions, the Drop Pod embarkation galleries and the upper observation/control galleries sit at **Brotherhood Deck elevation**, while much of the deployment ground is **one working level lower**.

## 7.4.1 Four Crusader Brotherhood residential complexes

Four broad **Crusader Brotherhood complexes**, arranged in mirrored pairs, flank the central Astartes thoroughfare. These are **future accommodation suites**. They do not mean four 30-Marine units were already present at founding, and their draft berths are **not automatically identical** to the now-agreed future structure of **four Crusading Squads of 20**.

### Typical Brotherhood barracks — the entrance view

The entrance opens into **one overall living chamber**, not two sealed dormitories. To **port/left**, the working original design places **twenty Neophyte sleeping berths**, organized as paired bunks with personal lockers and equipment storage. The **central shared floor** is deliberately roomy, with communal tables and space for ordinary conversations and study.

To **starboard/right**, a dividing wall with a **central open arch** leads into the Initiates' semi-private end of the same living area. There are **ten single berths in alcoves**, **five on each side** of the aisle, with low partitions for modest privacy. There are **no exterior windows** in these sleeping rooms. The design was revised to preserve a roomy central living space rather than a cramped maze of separated little rooms.

**Connection:** Central Astartes corridor ↔ single Brotherhood barracks ↔ shared equipment/hygiene passage immediately beyond the main sleeping room.

### Equipment and hygiene passage

Straight ahead from the barracks is a **locker, equipment and hygiene passage**. Armor and personal equipment preparation are accommodated here rather than scattered through the sleeping positions. It is the principal controlled transition from daily quarters to the weapons and training rooms.

**Connections:** Barracks ↔ equipment/hygiene passage ↔ dedicated squad armory; the adjacent sparring room also has direct access to the armory, avoiding a detour through the sleeping area.

### Dedicated squad armory

A private **small-unit armory** maintains and secures routine wargear for the brothers assigned to the suite. It is not the ship's **Techmarine forge**, nor the Marshal's Private Arsenal.

**Connections:** Equipment passage ↔ armory ↔ **adjacent squad sparring room**. A brother can draw a practice weapon and enter the training room without taking it back through the barracks.

### Squad sparring hall

Each Brotherhood has **one practical sparring room**, sized in the original concept for around **ten to fifteen Marines at a time**. It supports everyday drills, weapon handling, instruction and small-group combat practice.

**Connections:** Armory ↔ sparring hall; dedicated squad training access leads downward toward the **two main combat arenas**. It is **not** the ceremonial Hall of Blades in the Sanctum.

### Supervising Sword Brother's chamber

A **compact private chamber** adjoins the armory and sparring spaces for the veteran serving as mentor or guide. The working concept envisages **four** such chambers—one for each Brotherhood. The Sword Brother remains a **member of the Marshal's Household** even when he is the on-duty mentor here. As discussed in our organizational work, the exact command and mentorship arrangements can be refined without inventing a separate company staff.

**Connection:** Immediate training/armory zone ↔ supervising Sword Brother's chamber; a controlled route to the **shared training Command and Control gallery** described below. Whether the same veteran also has a general veteran room remains **open**; we must not count him twice in the active roster.

!!! info "Residential capacity versus the actual organization"
    The **original architectural layout** gives each suite **20 Neophyte + 10 Initiate berths** (nominal room capacity: **30**); across four suites, **80 Neophyte + 40 Initiate berths**. Our *later approved military plan* is **four Crusading Squads of 20 total** (one Sword Brother + 19 Initiates/Neophytes per squad). The ship may retain spare capacity, or we may revise the bed counts later. **The 30-berth draft is not a current squad roster.**

## 7.4.2 Shared training control galleries

Between each **mirrored pair of Brotherhoods** a smaller connecting passage serves a **training-only Command and Control (C&C) gallery**. The design has **three entrances**: one from the first supervising Sword Brother's chamber, one from the second, and one from the shared corridor.

These galleries **look down into the two enormous combat arenas on the lower Hangar and Logistics Deck**. They coordinate simulated exercises, terrain and observation—not voidship navigation or live military command. Their sightlines and exact equipment remain for future mapping.

**Connections:** Brotherhood A mentor chamber ↔ training C&C ↔ Brotherhood B mentor chamber, plus the shared corridor; C&C visual/controlled access toward the lower arena.

## 7.4.3 Port and starboard veteran wings

Farther along the main residential corridor are **two matching veteran wings**. Each is a narrow but usable corridor with **fifteen individual private chambers**, positioned on both sides of its passage, for **thirty rooms in all**. These may eventually accommodate Sword Brethren, other veterans and an Ancient or banner bearer; many may stay empty.

A typical veteran cell contains one sleeping berth, personal shrine, storage for armor and arms, campaign honors and texts. Each wing has **one quieter contemplation room** for a few veterans to meet or study in private.

**Connections:** Main residential corridor ↔ port veteran corridor and starboard veteran corridor ↔ private cells and each wing's contemplation room. These are separate from the six Marshal's **household guards**, who quarter in the **Inner Sanctum above**.

## 7.4.4 Great Brotherhood Hall

The residential spine culminates at the **Great Brotherhood Hall**. Broad **open Gothic arches, without closing doors**, reveal one **continuous communal room** filled with long shared tables, devotional and campaign decoration, bookshelves and readable data-slates. The original concept targets seating for roughly **160 Marines**, including officers and guests as appropriate; this is long-term **architectural capacity**, not the current fielded number.

Here the brothers share food and drink, discuss tactics and doctrine, remember past wars, read Imperial histories, settle arguments and enjoy the comradeship that the stricter ceremonial sanctum does not afford. Available books and slates are **ordinary shared references**, not the restricted Grand Archives.

**Connections:** Main Brotherhood corridor ↔ **wide open arches** ↔ uninterrupted central hall. No public-through-route to the allied wings, and no subdivision into several separate halls.

## 7.4.5 Upper Thunderhawk berths and launch galleries

The **main hangar opens through this level**. One elevated **Thunderhawk berth lies port**, and one **starboard**, each with its own safe approach and flight clearance; these are the **upper two positions** of a **four-Thunderhawk working capacity concept**. The **lower two positions** are on the Hangar and Logistics Deck.

Immediately beside the upper Thunderhawk berths are the **port and starboard Drop Pod embarkation galleries**. These receive deploying Marines from the Astartes residential routes without sending everyone down through the cargo holds. Devotional alcoves, sigils and short prayer areas line the galleries.

The planned Drop Pod provision is **twelve pods in total**, **six launch-ready** (three per side) plus **six in reserve or preparation** (three per side). This does not imply twelve simultaneous launch tubes. A standard troop pod is nominally for ten power-armored Astartes; **six ready pods could carry up to sixty** if filled to nominal capacity, but this is a design calculation, not a recorded deployment.

**Connections:** Astartes living corridor ↔ upper launch gallery ↔ the local Thunderhawk berth and adjacent Drop Pod embarkation positions; reserve-pod maintenance and loading routes ↔ lower logistics. The exact tubes, reload rails and launch orientation are **not yet mapped**.

## 7.4.6 Port and starboard allied wings

Beyond secured gates outside the central Astartes precinct lie **two auxiliary wings**, each originally planned for approximately **200 visiting allied troops or personnel**. Barracks, smaller mess areas, washrooms, supply rooms, arms storage, exercise space and first-aid stations run along their separate residential corridors. This is accommodation for Imperial allies—not automatic permanent membership in the Sons of Sigismund.

A separate combined surge planning figure of **roughly 500 allied personnel** was discussed, with overflow elsewhere on ship. A possible package of **300 Astra Militarum plus 200 attached medical, technical and logistical personnel** is only a model, **not** a deployment that has already occurred. The 173rd Armored Rangers are a **possible recurring ally**, not a confirmed embarked garrison.

### Six Valkyrie-capable positions

Three small landing/servicing positions are proposed at Brotherhood elevation **port**, and three **starboard**, with direct controlled connection to the allied wings. These are **capacity/servicing positions**, not six owned or permanently assigned Valkyries.

**Connections:** Allied wing ↔ local aircraft positions; allied wing ↔ independent ramps/lifts descending to the lower hangar/cargo level and **Mortal Medicae**, **not** through the Astartes residential spaces. Physical aircraft clearances and servicing arrangements remain open.

## 7.4.7 Training access and normal daily movement

A brother wakes in his alcove, uses the equipment passage, draws wargear from the small armory, and practices in the sparring hall. For a larger exercise he takes the **dedicated squad training route** into the lower arena, observed by the training C&C gallery. For a sudden deployment he leaves by the main Astartes passage for a **Thunderhawk or Drop Pod** at Brotherhood elevation. For ordinary communal gathering he continues down the long residential spine to the **Great Brotherhood Hall**.

None of these routes requires crossing the Marshal's Inner Sanctum or the lower cargo floor. The allied barracks have their **own** access routes. This separation is essential if we later draw a playable, coherent deck map.
