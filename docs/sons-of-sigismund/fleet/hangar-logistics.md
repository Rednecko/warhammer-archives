# 7.5 Grand Hangar and Logistics Deck

<div class="archive-page-label">The Righteous Crusader · Principal deployment, training and industrial level</div>

<span class="archive-status">Functional areas and routes recorded</span> <span class="archive-status open">Actual equipment inventory and exact dimensions open</span>

A level beneath the main Astartes residential quarters, the **Grand Hangar and Logistics Deck** forms the cruiser’s practical warfighting base. Here the Sons' aircraft are prepared, armored vehicles loaded and repaired, troops embarked, supplies safeguarded, wounded received and recovered relics taken through examination before reaching the sacred collections above. Unlike the Sanctum's ceremonial passages, these are **armored industrial spaces** of equipment, blast doors, freight lifts and broad transit routes.

The hangar itself is an immense **multilevel void**; one cannot assign every pad and upper gallery to this lower literal floor. The lower two Thunderhawk positions and principal loading ground belong to this level. **Two further upper Thunderhawk berths, the Drop Pod embarkation galleries and Valkyrie-capable positions sit at Brotherhood elevation above**.

## 7.5.1 Lower Grand Hangar — two Thunderhawks

The broad central deployment platform is designed for **two Thunderhawks to stand and embark troops simultaneously**, with safe space for cargo haulers, servitors, ammunition carts, equipment crews and armored Marines. This is more than two small parking rectangles: loading, marshaling and departure must remain practical.

The **upper two Thunderhawk positions**—one port and one starboard—look into the same open volume, creating a **four-position planned complement** across both elevations. Visiting aircraft, evacuation craft and other allied transports may use flexible authorized lower areas if size and operations allow. The **actual permanent craft roster is not confirmed**.

**Connections:** Lower deployment floor ↔ protected embarkation/marshaling approaches; broad **reinforced vehicle roadway** ↔ motor pool and cargo holds. Personnel use **port and starboard routes** and twin grand lift banks. Freight and aircraft servicing do not rely on those main passenger lifts.

## 7.5.2 Twin grand lifts and observation landings

Two main personnel elevator banks flank the hangar—**port and starboard**—rather than dropping down the middle of the flight area. They connect important levels to observation positions overlooking the active floor. The most ornate observation position is Sanctum-facing, with armored glazing and room for a Chaplain to address deploying troops; the lower landing functions are deliberately more practical.

**Connections:** Port and starboard lift banks ↔ lower hangar pedestrian approaches ↔ Brotherhood and Sanctum levels. Separate heavy **vehicle/freight lifts, service routes and training access** carry cargo, crew and exercise equipment.

## 7.5.3 Central logistics corridor, cargo holds and magazines

A reinforced industrial thoroughfare leads **inward from the hangar platform** toward the main cargo system. It is wide enough for tanks, vehicle transport and heavy stores. Storage includes ordinary food, water, medical goods, spare machinery, replacement parts, ammunition and campaign supplies for both Astartes and their supporting mortal population. The source proposes **roughly one to two years of routine endurance under favorable, non-intensive conditions** as a *design aspiration*; actual volume, consumption and resupply assumptions have not been verified.

Dangerous ammunition and warheads belong in **protected segregated magazines**, not beside living quarters or casually next to the Techmarine's personal chamber. **Service and freight corridors** run independently of the primary pedestrian passages. The cargo route is a **logistical spine**, not a mandatory shortcut between every deck.

**Connections:** Hangar floor ↔ reinforced loading route ↔ cargo holds / motor pool; protected magazine branch routes and dedicated freight/vehicle lifts; lateral personnel corridors ↔ arenas and allied sections without passing through open storage yards.

## 7.5.4 Motor pool and vehicle staging

The motor pool is integrated with the **lower cargo complex**. It supports repairs, vehicle parking, preparation for landing and rapid recovery of damaged equipment. The design allows some temporary allied armor, including possible Leman Russ tanks and support vehicles, but **no actual number or confirmed 173rd Armored Rangers deployment** has been approved.

**Connections:** Main hangar vehicle roadway ↔ motor-pool and cargo staging; dedicated heavy vehicle lifts or handling routes ↔ appropriate launch areas (exact machinery and route geometry open).

## 7.5.5 Port combat training arena

One of two **separate double-height arenas** occupies the **port side of the lower hangar/cargo complex**. Its fighting floor is on this level; it extends upward to the **Brotherhood Deck's observation and training C&C gallery**. Terrain and obstacles can be reset via controlled logistics access.

The working design allows **multiple squads and perhaps eighty or more Marines across a major exercise** as a capacity goal—not a fixed dimension or current strength. Whether full vehicle-scale mock battles fit is open.

**Connections:** Dedicated squad stairs/lifts from Brotherhood sparring facilities ↔ port arena; lateral pedestrian corridor from the port hangar lift landing ↔ port arena; secured industrial entrance ↔ training equipment staging; upper observation ↔ port training C&C. This arena is not the **Hall of Blades**.

## 7.5.6 Starboard combat training arena

The **starboard arena** is independent of the port arena, so both can conduct exercises without interfering with each other. It has its own lower fighting floor, upper observation/control space and training equipment passages. Its general role and potential scale mirror the port arena; exact sightlines, dimensions and detailed training configurations are open.

**Connections:** Brotherhood sparring/training routes ↔ starboard arena; starboard personnel corridor beside the hangar ↔ arena entrance; secured logistics staging ↔ arena floor; upper control gallery ↔ training-only C&C.

## 7.5.7 Astartes Apothecarion

Near the **landing and casualty-reception route** sits a **full Astartes medical wing**, rather than one treatment room. The original ship draft calls for triage, several surgical stations, critical care, recovery berths, medical storage, protected gene-seed facilities and private quarters/offices for the Apothecary. The surge design target is **30–40 wounded Space Marines** to be accommodated or processed during a major emergency—not forty surgeries performed simultaneously, and **not** evidence of a named founding Apothecary.

**Connections:** Controlled Astartes casualty approach from hangar/reception ↔ triage ↔ surgery/critical care/recovery; protected internal access ↔ gene-seed and medical stores; staff circulation ↔ proposed private quarters.

## 7.5.8 Techmarine's forge and repair wing

Across the main industrial route is the **Techmarine's forge**, a substantial vehicle-scale repair and fabrication complex with armor maintenance, weapon servicing, machinery, workshops, spare components and a proposed private workroom. Mortal artificers, serfs and servitors may support these facilities. This is a working Astartes **equipment and relic-technical facility**—it is *not* the ship's main plasma engine or its entire engineering command.

**Connections:** Industrial corridor / motor pool ↔ forge vehicle and equipment bays; adjacent protected area ↔ **Relic Reception and Examination Complex**; deeper ship-engineering networks provide ordinary technical support without being part of the same room.

## 7.5.9 Relic reception, quarantine and examination

**Directly beside the forge**, with a separate secured approach from the cargo-reception side, is the relic-processing complex. A recovered item is **logged, isolated, authenticated and examined** before it may enter the order's collections. It comprises receiving/registration, examination rooms, quarantine areas, temporary secure storage and record-keeping. Dangerous or suspect artifacts remain contained.

A **Chaplain** oversees spiritual purity and sacred significance; a **Techmarine or qualified specialist** evaluates mechanisms, materials and suspicious technology. A Chaplaincy/ecclesiastical liaison workroom is a working proposal beside these facilities, not a confirmed permanent staff complement.

**Connections:** Protected cargo/relic reception ↔ secure relic rooms; examination ↔ forge technical facilities; authorized upward transfer ↔ Grand Archives, lesser protected collections or **High Relic Vault**, subject to approval. **No artifact goes directly from an open pad into the High Relic Vault.**

## 7.5.10 Mortal Medicae Wing

The ship carries a **separate medical complex for human patients**, supporting the permanent crew and embarked allies. This is the correct destination for wounded Guardsmen, allied evacuees and ship ratings. Small allied-wing first-aid posts may stabilize people, but the principal human medical wing provides larger surgery, recovery and mass-casualty support.

**Connections:** Valkyrie-capable upper allied positions ↔ independent casualty lifts/ramps ↔ lower Mortal Medicae Wing; hangar's controlled casualty-reception route ↔ human treatment. This path does **not** traverse the Astartes barracks, Grand Reclusiam or Inner Sanctum.

## 7.5.11 A deployment and relic-recovery walkthrough

Before deployment, the marines assemble in their quarters above, draw wargear and move through the **Brotherhood launch galleries** toward Drop Pods or Thunderhawks. At the lower level, heavy equipment teams load vehicles, ammunition and cargo along the reinforced roadway. After a raid, casualties are diverted immediately to the proper Astartes or mortal medical route; cargo and artifacts proceed to the protected reception chain. Relics are not pronounced sacred by simply arriving aboard—the receiving and examination process comes first.

!!! note "Design capacities, not the current fleet inventory"
    The **four Thunderhawk positions, twelve Drop Pods (six ready/six reserve), six Valkyrie-capable pads, large training arenas, and 30–40-patient medical surge target** describe working planned capacity. They do not establish that all these vehicles, pads, or medical posts exist as a staffed and fully stocked founding order of battle.
